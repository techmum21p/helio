# Solar Installation Opportunity Report: Laguna
**Location:** Laguna, Philippines | **Date:** May 28, 2026

## Executive Summary
The province of Laguna presents a total addressable market across its top 20 municipalities, with a clear concentration of high-opportunity zones in its northern industrial and southern urban corridors. The strongest potential lies in commercial and industrial installations within the 1st and 2nd class cities of **Bay, City of Santa Rosa, and City of Cabuyao**, driven by high economic activity and institutional clients. The key recommendation is to prioritize a targeted sales strategy focusing on commercial establishments and industrial zones in these high-tier areas, while developing flexible financing models to address cost sensitivity in lower-income municipalities.

## Detailed Profiles

### 1. Liliw
Liliw presents a moderate opportunity due to decent solar irradiance and a base of local businesses, tempered by its 3rd-class income classification. The primary opportunity is to target tourism-linked establishments—restaurants, cafes, and lodging—to reduce operational costs and appeal to eco-conscious visitors, leveraging the town's reputation for crafts and culinary tourism. The key risk is high upfront cost sensitivity in the municipality, making accessible financing options critical to adoption despite clear long-term savings.

### 2. Kalayaan
Kalayaan offers strong potential thanks to exceptionally high solar irradiance (0.96) within an urbanizing municipality. The strategy should target residential subdivisions and local small businesses with lease-to-own or financing models, using the municipality's high solar yield to offer compelling payback periods. The major risk is the town's modest economic activity and 3rd-class income level, which may limit the capital available for solar investments and require a focus on financial accessibility.

### 3. City of San Pablo
The City of San Pablo is a promising market with good solar irradiance and a relatively high (2nd class) income classification, but its smaller, rural character suggests a niche approach. The opportunity lies in targeting established middle-income homeowners in subdivisions like Camella with rooftop solar to offset electricity costs and increase property value. The risk is a potentially limited total addressable market due to the small population, which could lead to high customer acquisition costs and rapid saturation.

### 4. Bay
Bay is a compelling market due to its 1st-class income classification, urban status, and host role to UPLB, which fuels strong local commerce and purchasing power. Targeted sales campaigns should focus on commercial establishments and boarding houses catering to the large student and faculty population. The risk involves competition for rooftop and ground space from other urban development in this high-density area, which could increase installation costs or limit available sites.

### 5. Luisiana
Luisiana is a densely populated, 3rd-class rural municipality with a strong agricultural base. While solar irradiance is sufficient, the limited disposable income of households is a primary constraint. The opportunity is to market small-scale, off-grid solar systems for residential lighting and agricultural water pumping to address energy needs not fully met by the grid. The economic profile suggests high price sensitivity, requiring strong financing or leasing models to overcome adoption barriers.

## Recommended Action Plan
1.  **Prioritize High-Tier Commercial Hubs:** Deploy an initial sales team to focus on industrial zones in **City of Cabuyao** and commercial districts in **City of Santa Rosa** and **Bay**, offering tailored commercial rooftop and ground-mount solutions.
2.  **Develop a Flexible Financing Suite:** Partner with local financial institutions to create lease-to-own and installment purchase products specifically for the residential and small commercial segments in **MEDIUM** tier municipalities like **Kalayaan** and **Liliw**.
3.  **Execute a Phased Pilot Program:** Launch a pilot program in **City of San Pablo** (high income, moderate scale) to test a referral and installation model for subdivision homeowners, refining the approach before expanding to similar residential markets.

## Methodology Note
This report synthesizes municipal-level data on solar irradiance estimates, population demographics, urban classification, and economic indicators (including income class) to calculate a composite opportunity score for each target area. Assessments and opportunities are derived from correlating these quantitative scores with qualitative market characteristics.
