**Date:** May 28, 2026  
**Analysis Region:** Province of Laguna, CALABARZON

## Executive Summary
An analysis of Laguna has identified a strong solar installation market, with 10 municipalities classified as HIGH or MEDIUM opportunity based on solar resource, economic potential, and market readiness. The top target is **Liliw**, which combines excellent solar irradiance with existing market traction and a supportive local government. The key recommendation is to **prioritize commercial and partnership-driven sales in the top-tier municipalities**, starting with Liliw, while developing accessible financing solutions to overcome income-related barriers in lower-class areas.

## Detailed Profiles

**1. Liliw**  
Liliw presents the strongest market entry point due to its exceptionally high solar irradiance (est. 1467 kWh/kWp/year) and confirmed solar adoption by MKSG SOLAR. The municipal government's 2025 mandate promoting locally-made products creates a favorable policy environment for partnership-driven sales. The primary opportunity is to position solar installations as a tool for households and businesses to support local economic development and achieve savings. The key risk is the municipality's 3rd-class income status, which indicates price sensitivity and necessitates carefully structured financing or payment solutions to broaden the market beyond early adopters.

**2. Luisiana**  
Luisiana offers an excellent solar resource and has already embraced solar for public infrastructure like streetlights. Its identity as the "Switzerland of Laguna" makes it a prime target for the tourism and hospitality sector, where solar can reduce operational costs and enhance eco-friendly branding. As a 3rd-class municipality, the primary risk is limited residential purchasing power; the commercial market may also be constrained by the dominance of SMEs, potentially leading to smaller average project sizes.

**3. Nagcarlan**  
Nagcarlan combines high solar potential with a moderately developed urban environment. The opportunity lies in targeting its agricultural sector (coffee and coconut processing) and tourism-related establishments with medium-scale rooftop systems to offset operational costs. The risk stems from its 3rd-class income classification, which could limit residential adoption. Effective financing solutions will be critical for penetrating the broader household market.

**4. Cavinti**  
Cavinti's exceptionally high solar irradiance and established ecotourism and adventure destination profile create a natural market for solar solutions targeting eco-resorts, glamping sites, and adventure parks. Solar can serve off-grid amenities and reduce operating costs while supporting sustainability branding. The key risk is the cyclical nature of tourism, which may make the commercial market vulnerable during off-peak seasons and could strain the cash flow of small tourism-dependent businesses despite favorable solar conditions.

**5. Magdalena**  
Magdalena benefits from above-average irradiance and signs of growing residential development, such as the Simplicio Green Homes project. The most direct opportunity is forming partnerships with developers of eco-themed subdivisions to integrate solar installations as a standard or premium feature for new homes. The market risk is the municipality's dual economy; while some areas show development, the core population's 3rd-class income status may lead to slow residential adoption due to limited disposable income for large upfront investments.

## Recommended Action Plan

1.  **Initiate Pilot Partnership in Liliw:** Immediately engage with the Liliw municipal government and local business associations (like the chamber of commerce) to explore partnerships under its pro-local business mandate. Propose a pilot program or a joint seminar to educate businesses and residents on solar benefits, using the successful installation by MKSG SOLAR as a case study.
2.  **Develop a Flexible Financing Offering:** Create a dedicated financing or leasing package for residential and small commercial customers in 3rd-class and 5th-class income municipalities (e.g., Liliw, Luisiana, Nagcarlan, Majayjay). Partner with local rural banks or microfinance institutions to make this accessible. This is essential to unlock volume in price-sensitive segments.
3.  **Create a Tourism Niche Sales Kit:** Develop a tailored proposal and case study package targeting the tourism and hospitality sectors. This should be deployed in municipalities with strong tourism identities like **Luisiana** ("Switzerland of Laguna"), **Cavinti** (ecotourism), and **Majayjay** (natural attractions), emphasizing reduced operational costs and enhanced sustainability marketing.

## Methodology Note
This analysis synthesizes data from multiple sources to generate the final opportunity scores. Key inputs include solar irradiance estimates (kWh/kWp/year), municipal income classification from the Department of Finance, urban/rural status classification, population data, and qualitative evidence of market readiness such as existing solar installations, government initiatives, and key economic drivers identified from public commercial and tourism listings.
