# Solar Installation Opportunity Report: Laguna

## Executive Summary
Laguna presents a diversified solar market opportunity across 10 top-ranked municipalities, led by the high-tier potential of **Bay** and the balanced profile of **Liliw**. While solar irradiance is generally favorable (1,468 - 1,731 kWh/year), the primary market driver is the economic capacity of each area, not just sunlight. The recommended approach is a dual strategy: focus premium offerings on high-income urban centers (Bay, City of Cabuyao) and develop targeted, cost-effective solutions for price-sensitive, high-potential municipalities like Liliw and San Pablo.

## Detailed Profiles

**1. Liliw**  
As a third-class municipality with a solid solar estimate (1,628 kWh/kWp/year) and urban status, Liliw offers a viable market centered on its footwear industry and tourism. The opportunity lies in targeting small manufacturers and tourism establishments with commercial solar solutions. The key risk is the potential affordability barrier within this income class, which may slow residential uptake.

**2. Kalayaan**  
Kalayaan benefits from excellent solar irradiance (1,731 kWh/kWp/year) and a strategic renewable energy context due to the nearby wind farm. The primary opportunity is to offer hybrid or complementary solar systems to an energy-aware community. However, the small population and moderate income level present a risk of slower sales cycles and a constrained customer base.

**3. Bay**  
Bay is classified as a **HIGH** tier market due to its top-tier (1st class) income, indicating strong purchasing power. While its solar irradiance is lower than some neighbors, the opportunity is clear: target affluent residential and commercial clients who prioritize energy independence and bill reduction over maximum generation. The risk is that lower solar output could make it less competitive compared to higher-yield locations.

**4. City of San Pablo**  
This 2nd-class city with good solar irradiance (1,650 kWh/kWp/year) has a proven market, evidenced by existing installer activity. The standout opportunity is a focused B2B approach, targeting the city's 11 commercial/industrial anchors. The market's limitation is its relatively small and rural population, which could increase customer acquisition costs for residential sales.

**5. Luisiana**  
Luisiana has a decent population and existing solar awareness but is a price-sensitive, 3rd-class market. The opportunity is to serve local agricultural and food processing SMEs with smaller, affordable solar solutions. The risk is that limited purchasing power may severely constrain the sales volume needed for profitability.

## Recommended Action Plan
1.  **Initiate Pilot Projects in Bay and Liliw:** Develop two distinct marketing packages: a premium ROI-focused package for high-income residents and businesses in **Bay**, and a commercially-oriented package for the small industrial and tourism sectors in **Liliw**.
2.  **Launch a Targeted B2B Outreach in San Pablo and Cabuyao:** Prioritize direct outreach to the malls, warehouses, factories, and commercial hubs in **City of San Pablo** and **City of Cabuyao** (a HIGH-tier adjacent city), leveraging their high daytime energy consumption.
3.  **Explore Partnership Models for Cost-Sensitive Markets:** For municipalities like **Luisiana, Calauan, and Los Baños**, research potential partnerships with local government units, agricultural cooperatives, or financial institutions to develop leasing or group-purchase schemes that mitigate the high upfront cost barrier.

## Methodology Note
This report synthesizes geospatial data (solar irradiance, urbanization, population density) with municipal economic classifications and market intelligence to score and rank potential. The final opportunity score balances solar potential with estimated purchasing power and market receptivity.
