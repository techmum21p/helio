## Executive Summary

The **City of Santa Rosa** emerges as the highest-priority target (Tier: HIGH), combining strong municipal "green city" initiatives with significant commercial and residential development. The primary recommendation is to focus sales efforts on commercial and institutional clients in the province's urban and industrial centers, leveraging local government renewable energy policies to build credibility and access projects.

## Detailed Profiles

**1. Liliw (MEDIUM)**: Liliw presents a viable market due to its solid solar irradiance (est. 1628 kWh/kWp/year) and substantial urban population. The local economy is anchored by tourism-linked businesses like restaurants (e.g., Sulyap Gallery Cafe). The key opportunity is targeting commercial clients in the tourism sector and municipal buildings, which value a positive "green" image. The primary risk is the municipality's 3rd-class income status, which may limit residential adoption without strong financing options.

**2. Kalayaan (MEDIUM)**: Kalayaan has excellent solar irradiance (1,731 kWh/kWp/year) and is influenced by the Kalayaan 2 Wind Farm, signaling strong institutional support for renewables. An opportunity exists to offer complementary solar solutions to project stakeholders and leverage the local green energy momentum. The risk involves competition from established national solar companies already present in the market.

**3. Bay (MEDIUM)**: Bay is a first-class urban municipality with high commercial energy demand from its hot spring resort industry. The opportunity is to target these resorts with solar to reduce operational costs. The risk is that its lower solar irradiance (1,468 kWh/kWp/year) may make the ROI less compelling for some buyers, despite the area's higher income level.

**4. City of San Pablo (MEDIUM)**: San Pablo offers favorable solar irradiance (1,650 kWh/kWp/year) and tourism appeal as the "City of Seven Lakes." The opportunity lies in serving resorts, farms, and agro-processing businesses. A critical risk is contaminated web data mixing results with San Pablo, California, requiring careful local verification of all intelligence.

**5. Luisiana (MEDIUM)**: Luisiana has a high population density and demonstrated solar adoption in public infrastructure (e.g., streetlights). The opportunity is to target its numerous local SMEs with cost-effective solar solutions. The risk is its modest solar irradiance, which may result in less compelling financial returns compared to higher-resource areas.

## Recommended Action Plan

1.  **Prioritize Commercial & Institutional Sales in HIGH-Tier Cities**: Immediately assign sales teams to the **City of Santa Rosa**, **City of Cabuyao**, and **Los Baños**. Focus outreach on factory/warehouse owners in Cabuyao, commercial developers in Santa Rosa's Nuvali area, and the administration of UPLB in Los Baños. Use the cities' own "green city" and academic goals as a conversation opener.

2.  **Develop a Tourism Sector Package**: Create a standardized solar proposal targeting hotels, resorts, and restaurants in key tourism municipalities like **Bay** (hot springs) and **Liliw** / **San Pablo**. The package should emphasize bill reduction for energy-intensive operations and the marketing benefit of a "green" brand.

3.  **Forge Financing Partnerships for Price-Sensitive Areas**: For medium-tier municipalities with high population but lower income (e.g., **Luisiana**, **Calauan**), proactively partner with local rural banks or microfinance institutions to develop accessible solar loan or rent-to-own products. This is essential to unlock the residential and SME market in these areas.

## Methodology Note
This report synthesizes a multi-factor **geo-score** based on solar irradiance potential, municipality income class, urban/rural status, and population data. The **final score** and tier ranking incorporate additional qualitative analysis of local market activity, existing competition, and alignment with renewable energy policies identified from publicly available intelligence.
